Problem Definition¶

American companies are depending more and more on foreign talent to fill labor shortages in a variety of areas. The Office of Foreign Labor Certification (OFLC) is in charge of reviewing an increasing number of visa applications annually to ascertain if firms may engage foreign workers without negatively affecting the pay or working conditions of American workers. It takes more time and resources to carefully evaluate every case as the number of applications keeps growing.

The goal of this project is to create a machine learning-based classification model that forecasts whether a visa application will be approved or rejected based on employer and applicant attributes like employment region, education level, job experience, prevailing wage, and company size. This methodology seeks to help decision-makers prioritize applications with a better chance of acceptance and indicate situations that might need more examination by identifying the critical elements that affect visa approval decisions.

From a business standpoint, the solution may shorten processing times, increase operational effectiveness, and offer data-driven insights into the profiles most likely to be certified for a visa. In order to ensure that the model promotes fair and informed decision-making rather than taking the place of human judgment, the final model is assessed using a variety of performance measures, with a focus on recall and overall prediction dependability.

Model Building¶

Multiple classification models were trained on the original (imbalanced) dataset to establish baseline performance. Tree-based and ensemble models were selected due to their ability to capture non-linear relationships. Model performance was evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC.

Original Data¶

Using the original (unbalanced) dataset, many classification models were constructed in this part to determine baseline performance. Because they can handle heterogeneous data types and nonlinear relationships, tree-based and ensemble models were chosen. Accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC were used to assess the model's performance.

In the majority of assessment measures, ensemble models like Random Forest, Bagging, and Boosting perform better than a single Decision Tree. Due to class imbalance, recall for the minority class is still comparatively poor, suggesting that the model's capacity to accurately identify cases of rejected visas is constrained by the original dataset. This is why resampling techniques are used in the following sections.

To ensure stable training and avoid memory issues from high-dimensional encoding, models were trained using a reduced numerical feature set.

Hyperparameter Tuning¶

The best-performing models were chosen among the models constructed on the original, oversampled, and undersampled datasets based on how well they balanced recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC. Models with continuously high recall and steady overall performance were given priority since the main goal is to accurately identify visa outcomes while managing class imbalance.

These factors led to the selection of Random Forest, AdaBoost, and Gradient Boosting for hyperparameter tweaking.

Models Selected for Adjustment¶

  1. Strong and reliable performance across datasets with a high recall and F1-score was shown by Random Forest.
  2. AdaBoost is a useful method for detecting minority classes since it achieved the highest recall in both oversampled and undersampled conditions.
  3. With competitive ROC-AUC performance, Gradient Boosting offered a robust balance between recall and F1-score.

As a result, these models were chosen for more optimization.

Actionable Insights & Recommendations¶

Model Performance Comparison¶

Ensemble approaches consistently performed better than single decision trees across all modeling techniques, especially when dealing with class imbalance. AdaBoost and Gradient Boosting, two boosting-based models, performed well in recognizing approved visa applications, as seen by their greatest recall values. A trade-off between sensitivity and false positives was highlighted by the fact that oversampling significantly increased recall but also decreased precision and accuracy. Gradient Boosting is the most dependable model for this use case as it offered the greatest overall balance between recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC.

Key Takeaways for Business¶

According to the investigation, employer and candidate characteristics including prevailing salary levels, firm size, and company age have a significant impact on visa certification outcomes. By giving instances with a better chance of acceptance priority, a machine learning-based screening method can drastically cut down on the amount of manual labor needed to assess applications. By using the customized Gradient Boosting model as a decision-support tool, OFLC can reduce the possibility of wrongly rejecting competent applicants while streamlining processing. To guarantee fairness and adherence to legal requirements, the model should be utilized in conjunction with human evaluation.
